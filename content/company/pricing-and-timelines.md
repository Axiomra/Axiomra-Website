# Pricing, timelines and how engagements work

## How much does a proof of concept (PoC) cost?

The exact price depends on the scope, the state of your data and how many systems the PoC has to connect to. You get a fixed price for the phase after a discovery call, never an open-ended hourly bill.

## How much does a production AI system cost?

A production system, meaning the model plus the interface people use, the integrations and a deployment pipeline, typically costs between $60k and $250k. It is priced as a fixed amount per phase once discovery is done. These are typical ranges; an exact quote always comes after discovery.

## What does the online cost estimator show?

The cost estimator on the homepage gives a directional range based on project type, complexity, size and timeline. It is a starting point for budgeting, not a quote. The fixed price comes after a scoping call.

## Are there ongoing costs after launch?

Yes. Once a system is live there are running costs for model inference, hosting and monitoring. Axiomra models these costs during discovery, so you know the expected monthly cost before the build starts.

## How long does a proof of concept take?

A proof of concept usually takes four to six weeks from kickoff to a working demo.

## How long does an MVP or production system take?

A minimum viable product (MVP) or production system, with a real interface, integrations and a deployment pipeline, usually takes three to six months. Smaller MVPs sit at the shorter end; systems with many integrations or strict compliance needs sit at the longer end. The first working slice is demoed within the first month.

## What happens in the first month of a project?

- Week one: discovery on your data, systems and constraints.
- Weeks two and three: a working slice of the highest-risk part of the project.
- Week four: a demo and a revised plan based on what was learned.

## How is the work run week to week?

Work runs in two-week cycles with a re-prioritised backlog. You see a working demo every week, get a written summary every Friday and have access to a shared project board. Anything that would move the end date is flagged in writing before the work starts.

## How is success measured?

One primary success metric is agreed before the build begins, and every demo reports progress against it. Accuracy targets are set together and tested for feasibility in the first phase.

## When does an AI project pay for itself?

Automation projects usually pay back within two quarters of going live. Projects aimed at growing revenue usually take longer to pay back.

## Who works on the project?

A typical team is three to six people: a lead engineer, one or two specialists, a product designer when there is an interface, and a technical project manager. You meet the team before signing, and the engineers who scope the project are the ones who build it. Teams keep four hours of daily overlap with your working day, with async written updates for the rest.

## What happens after launch?

You can take a full handover with a runbook, or keep Axiomra on a support retainer that covers monitoring, model drift, upgrades and a response-time commitment. You own the code, models, weights and cloud accounts from the first commit.
